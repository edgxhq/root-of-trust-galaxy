In 2017 companies and mining pools representing most of Bitcoin's computing power signed an agreement to raise
the block size. People running nodes did not go along with it, and the change was abandoned that November.

**What it shows.** A root can withstand pressure from its own largest participants when those who verify are
many and independent. It also shows the cost: the outcome was decided off-chain, and there is no record of who
decided.
