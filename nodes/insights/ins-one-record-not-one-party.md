The objection is fair to raise: one root sounds like the opposite of decentralisation. It runs two things together.
One root is one shared record. Decentralisation is about how few parties could control that record, link by link,
which is what [[method-shortest-stave]] counts.

Agreeing on one record is the job a root of trust does. In 2021 Cardano's founder, describing what Bitcoin's
supporters believe it solved, called it "this big decentralized root of trust, one canonical ordering for the events of
the world, the monies of the world, one version of truth that everybody can deterministically verify themselves".
Separate roots for separate facts can make sense. Two roots for the same facts bring back the question of which one to
believe, and move the trust to whatever stands between them ([[claim-one-root]]).

So the work for one root is not to multiply it but to spread control of it: many operators, more than one node
implementation, rules set by recorded vote, more than one kind of resource securing it
([[multi-resource-consensus]]), and independent eyes on it ([[q-partner-chains-watching-the-root]]). The quotation was
checked by ear on 25 November 2026.
