When the same asset is settled on two ledgers that are governed separately, something has to stand in the
middle and vouch for both. The trust that the two ledgers earned does not carry across; it rests on the thing
in between.

**For.** Of US$3.1 billion stolen from decentralised finance protocols in 2022, 64% came from cross-chain
bridges. In July 2026 a bridge carrying NIGHT to another chain was compromised while the Cardano side was
untouched. The web's many certificate roots failed at their weakest in 2011.

**Against, or limiting.** The 2022 figure is the peak year and a subset of all thefts. Vitalik Buterin argued
in 2022 for several sovereign ledgers that are not welded together, which is a case for many roots kept
apart, not for one. And more than one kind of resource can secure a single ledger and make it stronger; the
claim is about spreading the same facts across ledgers, not about how one ledger is secured.
