Pseudo-public infrastructure is open to anyone and owned by no one outright, but its public does not hold the
decisions that steer it. They are made off the ledger, by agreement among developers, companies or
foundations, or they rest on a short list. So it is public in use and not in ownership, and the label says so.

A root is placed here when it does not pass the test for [[tier-public]]. Today that is Bitcoin, Ethereum,
Solana and the XRP Ledger:

- Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana change their rules by agreement reached off the ledger, so there is no recorded
  vote to count.
- The XRP Ledger records its votes on rule changes, but who votes rests on lists published by two
  organisations.
- For none of the four has this research found upkeep paid by a vote recorded on the ledger.

The four are also less decentralised than Cardano on the one independent measure that covers several of them:
of the four chains the Edinburgh index scores on all five of its layers, Cardano has the highest average and
the highest lowest layer ([[claim-cardano-highest-lowest-layer]]). Solana and the XRP Ledger are not scored on
all five.

"Pseudo" is a statement about who decides, not about how well these networks run or how much they are used. A
root drops the word when it passes the test.
